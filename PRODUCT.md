# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Jazz Angels is a Long Beach / Signal Hill 501(c)(3) that teaches jazz to middle- and high-school
musicians through ensemble playing. The portal is internal tooling for a very small office:

- **Barry Cogert, Program Director.** Owns most grants, leads ensembles on Sundays, approves
  timesheets. Uses the portal in short bursts between rehearsals, often on a laptop at the studio.
- **Denise Moreno, Office Administrator.** Keeps the grant folder, the roster, and payroll straight.
  The most frequent user; lives in tables and forms.
- **Teaching artists (Albert Alva, Devon Price, Renee Cole).** Take roll on a phone or tablet at the
  bandstand, log hours weekly. Low patience for chrome; want the one thing they came for.
- **A board member.** Reads the dashboard before a meeting to see the pipeline and the numbers.

Everyone is a musician or works for musicians. Nobody is an operations professional. The job is
"know what is due, do the next thing, and have the numbers ready when a funder asks".

## Product Purpose

One portal for the office's workflows, built as a platform: a shared core (people, programs,
fiscal year, settings) and a module per workflow. Today: **Grants** (the lifecycle from prospect to
closed, deadlines, money, the Playbook of checklist templates), **Teaching** (the class schedule,
roll call, the student roster), and **Timesheets** (teaching-artist hours and approvals). The
modules share the same nouns so a grant report can pull attendance and hours without anyone
retyping them.

Success looks like: the program director opens the dashboard on a Sunday and sees, in one place,
what is due this week across grants and classes, and a funder report can be written from numbers
the portal already has.

## Brand Personality

Warm, plain, specific. Institutional "we". Numbers do the bragging. Jazz vocabulary used
unapologetically (charts, combos, big band, mentors). Portal copy is even plainer than site copy:
labels are nouns, buttons are verbs, empty states say what will appear and how to make it appear.
Three words: **steady, practical, musical.** The visual direction is "mid-century geometric,
Blue Note record sleeve, tidied up": flat colour, geometric display type, warm paper neutrals.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS admin templates: gradient hero metrics, identical icon-card grids, glassmorphism.
- Nonprofit "impact" dashboards with donut charts and superlatives. No "unlock / empower /
  transform", no exclamation stacking, no "it's not just X, it's Y".
- Emoji anywhere. The public site uses them; the portal deliberately drops them.
- Cold, blue-grey enterprise neutrals. This brand's greys are paper-warm.
- Anything pill-shaped or bouncy. Restrained radii, steady motion, no spring.

## Strategic Design Principles

1. **The next thing is always visible.** Every screen answers "what is due, what do I do now".
2. **One vocabulary across modules.** A Badge, a table row, a stat, a dialog look the same whether
   the module is grants, teaching or timesheets. Consistency is the affordance.
3. **Gold is rationed.** Attention states and the "Awarded this FY" rule only. If everything is
   gold, nothing is.
4. **Density where people work, air where people read.** Tables and rosters are dense; the
   dashboard and detail pages breathe.
5. **Numbers over adjectives.** Counts, dollars, hours, dates. Mono type for anything that lines up.
6. **The portal should disappear into the task.** Familiar patterns (rail, top bar, tabs, tables),
   no invented affordances.

## Accessibility

WCAG AA contrast on all text. Visible 3px focus ring on every control. Roll call must be usable
one-handed on a phone at the bandstand: large targets, no hover-only affordances. Respect
`prefers-reduced-motion`; nothing loops or pulses anyway.
